= Governance — Authority Watch
:toc:
:icons: font

[.lead]
This document defines the governance structure, roles, responsibilities, and
decision-making processes for the Authority Watch project.

== Safety Authority and Trust Model

=== Core Principle

*The source publisher controls what was published. Authority Watch only records
that publication.*

This is the foundational trust boundary: Authority Watch does not create,
modify, or interpret authoritative content. It only observes, records, and
tracks the state of official publications.

=== Trust Chain

. *Publisher*: The official source (e.g., legislation.gov.uk) publishes content
. *Observer*: Authority Watch records the publication (observation)
. *Reviewer*: A qualified human interprets what the publication means (interpretation)
. *Approver*: A separate human authorizes the interpretation (approval)
. *Signer*: A trusted human signs the approved bundle (signing)
. *Publisher*: Authority Watch publishes the signed bundle (publication)
. *Consumer*: Downstream systems consume the published bundle (consumption)

*Critical:* Signing attests to the approved bundle — it does *not* create
legal truth. The legal truth is established by the source publisher.

== Review Requirements

=== Production Legal-Rule Changes

Production legal-rule changes *require* two identified, independent reviewers.

*Each reviewer must:*

. Have documented qualifications for the jurisdiction
. Review the source document and its provenance
. Review the interpretation and its reasoning
. Review the effective date determination
. Document uncertainties and assumptions
. Sign the review record with their identifier

=== Provisional Mode

During private solo experimentation, one curator may produce bundles marked
`PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_REVIEW`.

*Constraints on provisional bundles:*

. Cannot support an accredited assessment claim
. Cannot support a professionally validated assessment claim
. Must be clearly marked as PROVISIONAL in all outputs
. Cannot be used for production decisions
. Must still pass all automated checks (tests, linting)

=== Review Process

. *Observation*: Document is ingested and hashed
. *Retrieval*: Document is fetched from official source
. *Source Verification*: Document provenance is verified
. *Parsing*: Document is parsed into structured format
. *Diffing*: Changes from previous version are computed
. *Machine Classification*: Automated classification of change type
. *Triage*: Human determines if change requires review
. *Impact Candidate Preparation*: Human prepares impact assessment
. *First Review*: First reviewer completes review
. *Second Review*: Second reviewer completes review (required for production)
. *Approval*: Change is approved for testing
. *Testing*: Automated and manual tests pass
. *Signing*: Bundle is signed with trusted key

== Publication Rules

A bundle *must not* activate if ANY of the following are true:

* It is unsigned
* It has an untrusted signature
* Tests have not passed
* Compatibility has not been declared
* Effective dates are not explicit
* It is not rollback-safe
* It has been revoked
* Review state is not `.signed` or `.published`
* Freshness is not `.current_verified` or `.current_provisional`

Operators remain responsible for professional and legal review of all
published bundles.

== Amendments and Corrections

Reviews and corrections are *append-only* events:

* A correction supersedes but does *not* erase its predecessor
* All previous versions remain in the ledger for auditability
* Each correction receives a new, unique identifier
* The superseded version is marked as superseded, not deleted

This ensures a complete audit trail of all changes and corrections.

== Role Definitions

[cols="1,2,3"]
|===
| Role | Responsibilities | Qualifications

| *Source Publisher* | Publishes official content | External (e.g., The National Archives)
| *Curator* | Configures and maintains profiles | Familiar with source APIs and terms
| *Reviewer* | Interprets documents and prepares reviews | Legal qualification for jurisdiction
| *Approver* | Authorizes interpretations | Senior legal qualification
| *Signer* | Signs approved bundles | Trusted key holder
| *Operator* | Manages system operations | Technical and security expertise
| *Maintainer* | Maintains codebase | Development and architectural expertise
| *Safety Officer* | Oversees safety-critical operations | Authority to halt operations
|===

== Decision-Making Process

=== Routine Decisions

Routine decisions (bug fixes, documentation updates, test additions) may be
made by maintainers with the following requirements:

. Code passes `just check` (lint, typecheck, test)
. Changes are reviewed by at least one other maintainer
. Changes do not modify safety-critical code paths

=== Safety-Critical Decisions

Safety-critical decisions (changes to `canAdvance`, `canActivate`, review
state definitions, activation gates) require:

. Two independent reviews by maintainers
. Explicit approval from the safety officer
. Updated documentation reflecting the change
. Updated tests covering the new behavior
. Entry in CHANGELOG.adoc describing the change

=== Architectural Decisions

Architectural decisions (new languages, major component additions, protocol
changes) require:

. Proposal in a GitHub Discussion or Issue
. Review period of at least 7 days
. Consensus among maintainers
. Documentation in ADR format (in META.a2ml)

== Conflict Resolution

Conflicts are resolved through:

. *Technical disagreements*: Majority vote among maintainers
. *Safety disagreements*: Safety officer has veto authority
. *Legal disagreements*: Escalation to legal reviewer
. *Process disagreements*: Escalation to project owner

== Code of Conduct

All contributors must follow the RSR Doctrine as stated in RSR-PHILOSOPHY.adoc:

* Holes before goals
* Always fail loudly
* Solutions at source

Additionally:

* Be respectful and professional in all communications
* Assume good intent
* Focus on the problem, not the person
* Document decisions, not just actions
